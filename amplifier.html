Projects / PROJ-2EI4-AMP

BJT Common-Collector Amplifier

2N3904Small-signal design LTSpiceSpectrum analysis ELECENG 2EI4

1.0Overview & Specification

The goal: design an amplifier with gain greater than 0.9 (attenuation under 10%) while maintaining linearity, driving a 100 Ω load from a source with 100 Ω output resistance. I chose an NPN BJT (2N3904) in common-collector (emitter-follower) configuration — the natural topology for a near-unity-gain buffer, achievable with the standard component kit.

2.0Hand Design

The design proceeded from the small-signal model, working backwards from the spec to component values:

  1. Transconductance. Enforcing linearity for inputs up to 0.5 V (0.2·VT(1 + gmRL) ≥ Vin) gives the requirement gm ≥ 990 mS.
  2. Input resistance of the transistor. With β = 100 (typical for the 2N3904), rπ = β/gm ≈ 101 Ω, so R′in = rπ(1 + gmRL) ≥ 10.1 kΩ.
  3. Bias network. The gain expression AV = [gmRL/(1 + gmRL)] · [Rin/(Rin + Rs)] ≥ 0.9 requires Rin ≥ 1 kΩ; solving the parallel combination for equal divider resistors gives R1 = R2 > 2.2 kΩ. I used 10 kΩ for comfortable margin.

3.0Simulation

A 10 ms transient simulation using the LTSpice 2N3904 model verified the design against a 1 kHz, 0.5 V sine input. Measured at the cursors: Vin peak of 499.99 mV against a Vout peak of 482.09 mV — a gain of 0.964 and attenuation of 3.58%, comfortably inside spec.

4.0Hardware Verification

The circuit was built on a breadboard and driven with the AD3. Peak measurements gave Vin = 498.71 mV and Vout = 453.67 mV — a gain of ~0.91 and attenuation of ~9%, inside the specification. The 5.6% deviation from simulation is attributable to component tolerances.

5.0Linearity Analysis

Linearity was verified three independent ways:

  • Spectrum plot. FFTs of input and output overlap closely across the band, with no significant harmonic content — minimal distortion.
  • Phase measurements. Input–output phase was measured at 1 Hz, 1 kHz, and 5 kHz; all values were small (0.06° at 5 kHz), showing the output tracks the input across frequency.
  • XY plot. Plotting Vout against Vin yields a tight, straight trace — the visual signature of a linear system.

Key result

A complete design loop closed on spec: hand calculation → simulation (0.964) → hardware (0.91), with linearity independently confirmed by spectrum, phase, and XY analysis.
