PROJ-2DX3-LIDAR EMBEDDED SYSTEMS

3D Indoor Spatial Mapper

A LIDAR-style scanner that produces 3D point-cloud maps of indoor spaces. A VL53L1X time-of-flight sensor is rotated through 360° by a 28BYJ-48 stepper motor while an MSP-EXP432E401Y microcontroller collects distance samples over I²C and streams them to a PC over UART, where MATLAB reconstructs and renders the scanned environment as a 3D point cloud.

Engineering challenges solved: electrical interference between the stepper driver and the I²C bus, reliable UART framing between firmware and MATLAB, and coordinate transformation from (angle, distance) samples into a consistent 3D space. Fully documented in an IEEE-style technical report.

PROJ-2EI4-XOR DIGITAL / CMOS

Transistor-Level XOR Gate — CMOS & Pass-Transistor

An XOR gate designed and built entirely from discrete MOSFETs, in two implementations. The first is a static CMOS design with complementary pull-up (PMOS) and pull-down (NMOS) networks, sized at a 2.5:1 PMOS:NMOS ratio to balance switching delays against hole vs. electron mobility. The second is a pass-transistor implementation of the same function.

Both circuits were verified with the AD3's logic analyzer across all input combinations, then characterized under a 100 nF capacitive load to extract rise, fall, and propagation delays. The static design was also shown to be fully bidirectional (Y = A ⊕ B and Y = B ⊕ A), with clean rail-to-rail levels (VH = 5 V, VL = 0 V).

PROJ-2EI4-SW ANALOG / MOSFET

MOSFET Analog Switch Design & Characterization

Two solid-state analog switches designed around PMOS/NMOS devices and evaluated against the properties of an ideal switch: on-resistance, off-state leakage, operating voltage range, and bidirectionality. Each design was simulated in LTSpice, built on a breadboard, and measured with the AD3.

The interesting part is the tradeoff: the single-PMOS design is simple and fully bidirectional but only operates from 2.5–5 V (limited by VGS > VT); the three-transistor design extends operation across the full 0–5 V range at the cost of bidirectionality. Choosing between them is a real engineering decision, and both were quantified to support it.

PROJ-2EI4-AMP ANALOG / BJT

BJT Common-Collector Amplifier

A buffer amplifier designed to meet a gain > 0.9 (attenuation < 10%) spec while maintaining linearity. Starting from the small-signal model, I derived the required transconductance (gm ≥ 990 mS), input resistance, and bias network values by hand, then selected a 2N3904 in common-collector topology.

The design was validated in three stages: LTSpice transient simulation, physical measurement on hardware, and linearity verification via spectrum analysis, phase measurements at 1 Hz / 1 kHz / 5 kHz, and XY plots. Simulation and hardware agreed within 5.6%, attributable to component tolerances.
