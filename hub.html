Centralized Learning Hub

Engineering design processStakeholder analysis Technical writingENGINEER 2PX3

1.0The Problem

Course content at McMaster is fragmented across several platforms — the LMS, Microsoft Teams, OneDrive, and email — with no consistency between courses. Students spend real time and attention just locating materials, deadlines, and announcements, and things get missed. This project applied a structured engineering design process to that problem, producing a concept for a centralized learning hub.

2.0Design Process

Unlike the circuit projects, the deliverable here was the process itself — practicing the methodology engineers use when the problem is fuzzy and the "users" are people:

  1. Stakeholder analysis — identifying students, instructors, TAs, and IT administration; mapping each group's needs and where they conflict.
  2. Requirements definition — translating those needs into concrete objectives, constraints, and evaluation criteria.
  3. Concept generation — structured brainstorming across solution approaches, captured in comparison tables.
  4. Evaluation & selection — scoring concepts against the criteria to converge on the centralized-hub design.
  5. Communication — a formal presentation and final report documenting the full rationale chain from problem to proposed design.

3.0What I Took Away

The value of this project was learning to defend design decisions with a traceable rationale: every feature in the final concept maps back to a stakeholder need through an explicit requirement. That discipline — requirements before solutions — carries directly into hardware work, where a spec-first mindset is what separates a design from a prototype.
